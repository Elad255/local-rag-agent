Espresso is a concentrated form of coffee brewed by forcing hot water under pressure through finely-ground coffee beans. It originated in Italy in the early twentieth century and remains the base for many popular drinks such as the cappuccino, latte, and americano.

The grind size for espresso is very fine, much finer than for drip coffee. If the grind is too coarse, water passes through too quickly and the shot tastes weak and sour; if it is too fine, the water struggles to pass through and the shot tastes bitter and over-extracted.

Water temperature matters as well. Most baristas aim for a brewing temperature between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius. Water that is too hot scorches the coffee and produces a burnt taste, while water that is too cool leads to a flat, under-extracted shot.

A standard single shot of espresso uses about 7 to 9 grams of coffee and yields roughly 30 milliliters of liquid in 25 to 30 seconds. A double shot, more common today, uses around 18 grams and yields about 60 milliliters. The golden ratio many baristas follow is roughly 1 part coffee to 2 parts liquid espresso.
